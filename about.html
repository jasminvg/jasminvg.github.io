About me

I was raised in Puerto Rico and Philadelphia, trained in anthropology and geography, and I organize for land justice in Kensington.

Bio

Jasmin Vélez

Jasmin Vélez (she/her) is a Puerto Rican practitioner-scholar, organizer, and researcher. Raised in Puerto Rico before moving to Kensington, Philadelphia, as a child, she has been a nearly lifelong resident of the neighborhood whose changes now shape her research.

She is a doctoral researcher in Geography, Environment & Urban Studies at Temple University, with a GIS certification. Her dissertation follows Puerto Ricans in Philadelphia and Puerto Rico through gentrification, displacement, and the vaivén of diaspora life.

For over a decade she has worked in community engagement, environmental justice, community gardening, and land justice across North Philadelphia. She holds an M.A. in Medical Anthropology from the University of Colorado Denver, where she studied land and identity with coffee-farming families in Puerto Rico, and a B.A. in Cultural Anthropology from Bloomsburg University of Pennsylvania.

Positionality

Positionality statement

I research the community I come from. As a Diasporican who lives and works in Kensington, I approach this work as a neighbor, an organizer, and a researcher.

I see the communities I work with as knowledge holders, and I aim for research that stays accountable to the people in it and returns to them in forms they can use.
